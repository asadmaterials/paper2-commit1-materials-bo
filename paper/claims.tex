\section*{S --- Scope statements the paper must make}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{S\arabic*},leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Exploratory history.} Use the plan's wording (\S0): benchmark, metrics and hypotheses were developed with access to outcome labels; the
analysis of the model-driven arms was registered (v1.0) and amended once (v1.1) before any such search was run for results. Splits r0--r2 were inspected.
\item \textbf{Scope.} One database snapshot (MP 2026.04.13), metallic multi-element structures, DFT elastic properties, a lookup oracle, one GP/EI
engine, 10\,+\,40 evaluations. No claim about experiments, stability-constrained search or other acquisition functions.
\item \textbf{Information asymmetry.} The historical prior uses the labelled history side of each split (about 1,000--1,100 materials); the GP starts from
10 points. The elemental-modulus prior uses no MP labels (handbook elemental values only). Differences between the two priors therefore reflect their
information sources as well as their form. Stated in the abstract-level framing and the Discussion, not only in Methods.
\item \textbf{Naming.} ``Elemental-modulus heuristic prior'': composition-only, structure-blind, not a model of compound moduli.
\item \textbf{\Sdis{} is prior-specific.} Recall levels are compared within a cell, never across priors.
\item \textbf{Outcome definition.} ``Sequential $T_{99}$'': first top-1\% hit among the 40 acquisitions, 41 if none. The gain is the ``reduction in restricted mean $T_{99}$'' (horizon 41), in acquisitions; never ``experiments saved'' or ``time to discovery'' without that qualifier. Same wording in the abstract, figures and Discussion.
\item \textbf{Estimand of the split-level test.} The 20 splits are independent random draws (each with its own seed) of a split of one fixed pool, so the split-level $t$ test is valid for the mean over random splits of \emph{this} pool, although splits share materials. It does not cover the variation from drawing a different pool of materials; that generalisation is a matter of scope (S2), not of the test.
