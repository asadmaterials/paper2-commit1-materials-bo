\snote{note:contents}{Organisation and provenance of the tables}

Section and table numbers without an S refer to the main text. The cells are the six prior--objective settings of the main text:
Y1 is the shear modulus $G_{\mathrm{VRH}}$ and Y2 the specific Young's modulus $E/\rho$; ``hist.'' is the historical prior,
``elem.'' the elemental-modulus heuristic prior, and ``m.'' the form matched to Y2. Unless a caption says otherwise, a table refers
to the primary universe (3,550 structures), estimates are means of the 20 split means (each the mean of three seeds), and
intervals are two-sided 95\% split-level $t$ intervals with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom (Section~\ref{M-sec:stats}).
Gains are reductions in the restricted mean sequential $\Tnn$, in acquisitions; $L$ is the coverage loss
$\Rdis(0)-\Rdis(\beta)$ in absolute recall.

Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:s-frontier}--\ref{tab:s-glmm} are outputs of the registered analysis (version~1.1; analysis
program \texttt{code/analyze\_runs.py}, SHA-256 beginning \texttt{\R{summary/analysis_sha}}), each labelled with the status it
was registered with: confirmatory (H1, H2), primary descriptive (the frontier), secondary, descriptive, diagnostic (engine
health) or exploratory. Supplementary
Tables~\ref{tab:s-censor}--\ref{tab:s-splittype} are post hoc: they were added after the registered analysis in response to
reviews of the draft and logged in the decision log; how far each specification preceded its results differs between them and is
stated in Supplementary Note~\ref{note:posthoc}.
No post hoc analysis changes a registered result.

Numbers are rounded half away from zero, and a negative value that rounds to zero keeps its sign ($-$0.0, $-$0.000), so that
an interval bound printed as $-$0.000 lies below zero. Every result in this document is generated rather than typed; the only numbers typed by hand are design constants (such as
the pool size) and the cross-platform reproducibility counts of decision-log entry~41. The tables are generated by \texttt{code/make\_supplement\_tables.py} from
\texttt{results/} and \texttt{results\_posthoc/}, and the numbers in the notes by \texttt{code/make\_numbers.py}. Where a table
shows only some weights, the remaining ones are in the file named in its caption.

\snote{note:posthoc}{Specifications of the post hoc analyses}

Five analyses were added after the registered analysis (decision-log entries 42--45). None involved new runs; all read the
confirmatory run records or the registered per-run outcomes (\texttt{results/run\_outcomes.csv}) and, except where stated, use
the registered split-level estimator. They are exploratory and are labelled post hoc wherever they appear; all but entry 44
are descriptive (no tests or decisions).

\paragraph{Entry 42: censoring and the initial design.} (i) The share of runs in which no top-1\% material was acquired within the
40 acquisitions, so that $\Tnn=41$, per universe, cell and weight (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s-censor}). (ii) The number of
split--seed blocks whose shared 10-point initial design already contains a top-1\% material, per universe and objective
(Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s-init}). In the registered outcome, $\Tnn$ counts acquisitions only, so a top-1\%
material in the initial design does not count (Section~\ref{M-sec:outcomes}). As a sensitivity check, every run of such a block was given $\Tnn=0$, which makes
its paired gain zero, and the one-sided lower 95\% bound of the gain was recomputed with the registered estimator. These three
quantities were defined in response to a review of the results and computed once; no specification was logged before they were
computed.

\paragraph{Entry 43: an interval for the loss of the absolute blind spot.} $\Sblind$ is the set of top-5\% materials below the
prior's 80th percentile, without the initial design; its pooled recall was registered as secondary, without an interval. The post
hoc quantity is $L_{\mathrm{blind}}(\beta)=R_{\mathrm{blind}}(0)-R_{\mathrm{blind}}(\beta)$, paired on split and seed, with the
per-run recall written by the registered analysis and the registered estimator and interval (Supplementary
Table~\ref{tab:s-blind}). No $p$-values, tests or decision rule. The specification was approved before any value was computed.

\paragraph{Entry 44: H2 with the beneficial bound also adjusted.} The registered H2 rule, unchanged except that a weight is
beneficial if the one-sided lower bound of the mean gain at level $1-0.05/6$ (the registered safety level) is above zero. With
both bounds at that level, the probability of declaring at least one weight of a cell beneficial and safe when none is, is at most
0.10 by the union bound. Computed for the 20\% margin and the registered descriptive 10\% and 25\% margins, in all three universes;
the registered decisions were recomputed from the same inputs and checked against \texttt{results/h2\_detail.csv}. The
specification was written after a preliminary computation of the three registered beneficial-and-safe sets with the adjusted
beneficial bound had been seen, as recorded in entry 44; its result is therefore not independent of that preview. Results:
Table~\ref{M-tab:h1h2}(b) and Supplementary Tables~\ref{tab:s-h2} and~\ref{tab:s-universes}.

\paragraph{Entry 45: estimates within each split type.} The gain and the coverage loss, paired on split and seed, estimated
within each split type as the mean of its 10 split means, with a two-sided 95\% $t$ interval on 9 degrees of freedom
($\mathrm{SE}=\sqrt{v/10}$, with $v$ the sample variance of the 10 split means); primary universe, all cells and weights
(Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s-splittype}). The specification was fixed before any value was computed. The mean of the two
split-type estimates equals the registered estimate, which is the equal-weighted mean of the two split types, so at least one of
the two always has the sign of the registered estimate. No $p$-values, tests or decisions, and no adjustment for the
\R{posthoc/splittype/n_all/primary} intervals; \R{posthoc/splittype/opposite/primary} of them lies entirely on the other side of
zero from the registered estimate (Section~\ref{M-sec:robust}).

\snote{note:glmm}{The candidate-level mixed model}

\paragraph{Specification (registered, secondary).} For each primary cell, one row per top-5\% material not in a run's initial
design, per run, for the eight runs of every split--seed block (one per weight, the $\beta=0$ run being the shared no-prior run):
\R{summary/glmm/n_rows_min}--\R{summary/glmm/n_rows_max} rows from \R{summary/glmm/n_runs_all} runs and
\R{summary/glmm/n_materials_all} distinct materials per cell. The response is whether the run acquired the material; the
covariates are the weight, as a factor with $\beta=0$ as reference, and the material's prior percentile within its split's
discovery pool. The model is the logistic mixed model
\[
\operatorname{logit}\Pr(\text{found}) = \text{weight} + \text{percentile} + \text{weight}\times\text{percentile}
 + u_{\text{run}} + v_{\text{material}},
\]
with independent normal random intercepts for run and material (in \texttt{statsmodels} formula notation,
\texttt{found \textasciitilde{} C(beta, Treatment('0')) * pctl} with variance components \texttt{0 + C(run)} and
\texttt{0 + C(material)}), fitted by \texttt{BinomialBayesMixedGLM.fit\_vb} with its default priors and a fixed random seed.
The main text uses only the sign and the ordering across weights of the weight$\times$percentile terms. A positive
term means that, relative to the no-prior search, the probability of finding a top-5\% material rises more steeply with its prior
percentile.

\paragraph{Result.} The posterior means of the weight$\times$percentile terms are in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:s-glmm}. They
are positive at $\beta=0.25$, 0.5, 0.75 and 1 in every cell, and increase with $\beta$ over those four weights. At
$\beta\le0.1$ their signs are mixed (negative for \cellB{} at 0.02--0.1 and for \cellF{} at 0.02). The model therefore agrees with
the shift shown in Fig.~\ref{M-fig:bins} in direction at $\beta\ge0.25$ only, which is all the main text uses.

\paragraph{Limitations.} We do not interpret the size of any coefficient. (i) Near-separation: at $\beta\ge0.5$ no top-5\% material in the prior's lowest half
is acquired in any cell, for two cells none is acquired there even at $\beta=0$ (Fig.~\ref{M-fig:bins}), and at $\beta=1$ none below
the prior's 95th percentile (Section~\ref{M-sec:where}). Several main effects and interactions are therefore very large and of
opposite sign, most of all at $\beta=1$, and the fit is close to the boundary of the parameter space. (ii)
Convergence: the variational fit reported non-convergence for \R{summary/glmm_nonconverged} of the 6 cells
(\R{summary/glmm_nonconverged_cells}). (iii) Uncertainty: the reported posterior standard deviations are mean-field
variational ones, which tend to be too small; they are not confidence intervals and are not shown. (iv) Reproducibility: the fit
is iterative, and on a second platform 95 of its 96 coefficients agreed to three decimals, against bit-for-bit agreement of every
deterministic output (Section~\ref{M-sec:runs}). (v) The runs of a split--seed block share their initial design and the
runs of a split share their discovery pool, but the model has no random effect for split or block. The full coefficient table, with posterior standard deviations,
is \texttt{results/glmm\_fixed\_effects.csv}; random-effect parameters and optimiser warnings are in
\texttt{results/summary.json}.
